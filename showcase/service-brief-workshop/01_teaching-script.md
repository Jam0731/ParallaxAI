# Write a service brief your AI can actually use

English mini-lesson · Estimated delivery time: 12 minutes, including practice and feedback.

**Audience:** Freelancers and consultants about to ask an AI assistant to make a service page. No prompt-engineering experience is needed.

**Learner result:** Turn a vague service pitch into a brief with six usable fields: buyer, concrete deliverables, boundaries, known price and timing, permitted proof, and contact destination. Preserve missing facts as visible questions. Completion means each field contains supplied facts or an explicit unresolved gap, with no invented claims.

**Promise boundary:** A clear brief can help make a service page understandable. This lesson does not establish demand or guarantee sales. It stops at the brief; no page, form, AI output or inquiry system is created here.

**Prerequisites:** Be able to edit a short document. Bring a service description if applying the lesson to your own work. The supplied fictional cases are enough to complete the lesson.

**Delivery assumption:** A recording or short live demonstration using any plain text editor. Screen directions below are production instructions, not reports of software actions already performed. No named platform, installation, account or purchase is required.

## Preparation outside the 12 minutes

- Instructor: open the worked example, learner worksheet and separate instructor key. Display only the relevant block of the worked example at each step. Keep the key hidden until feedback.
- Learner: open the worksheet or print it before starting. Allow approximately two minutes for file setup outside the advertised lesson time.
- The final Fieldwork brief also exists as a copy-ready text file. It is a fictional teaching artifact, not a publishable real-business brief.

**Exact displayed input:**

```text
Buyer:
Deliverables:
Boundaries and client inputs:
Price and timing:
Permitted proof:
Contact destination:

Each field needs a source fact or a visible question.
```

**Say:**

> Before you ask an AI assistant to make a service page, give it something more useful than “make me sound professional.” Give it the facts a buyer needs to understand the work. In twelve minutes, you will turn a vague pitch into a short service brief. We will use these six fields, then you will try a different example. A clear brief can help make the page understandable; it cannot guarantee sales. Both businesses in this lesson are fictional. Their contact addresses are examples, not real inquiry destinations.

**Expected visible result — proposed teaching artifact:** A blank structure with an explicit rule for gaps. No business or software functionality has been verified.

**Transition:** The template tells us what to collect. Now we need to decide which statements deserve to go into it.

## 2. Separate facts from a pitch — 00:50–02:00

**Screen action:** Show the fictional Fieldwork source card from the worked example beside this invented vague pitch. Underline “independent design studios with 3–12 people” on the source card. Strike through the unsupported claim below; leave the source card unchanged.

**Exact displayed input:**

```text
Illustrative vague pitch, invented for this lesson:
“We help creative teams work better with smooth, stress-free handoffs.”

Unsupported claim, invented for this lesson:
“We cut handoff delays in half.”

Question: Which supplied fact supports “in half”?
```

**Say:**

> Fieldwork Studio starts with this vague pitch: “We help creative teams work better with smooth, stress-free handoffs.” It sounds pleasant, but we cannot yet tell who should buy, what they receive, or where the work stops. The source card gives us more useful material. It names a specific buyer and a specific handoff: from approved design to implementation. Keep that. Now look at “We cut handoff delays in half.” Which source fact supports “in half”? There is none. Remove the claim. When a fact is missing, write “to confirm.” An AI assistant can arrange your evidence, but it should not supply evidence you do not have.

**Checkpoint:** Allow about five seconds for the source check, inside this segment. For a recording, display the question, then give the explanation already scripted above. For a live class, if someone says “it sounds plausible,” reply: “Plausible is not supplied. We need a result we can substantiate before using that claim.” If someone suggests changing “half” to “significantly,” reply: “That still claims a result we have not been given.”

**Expected visible result — constructed from supplied fictional facts:** The unsupported result claim is removed. The buyer and handoff context remain available for the next step.

**Transition:** We now have two reliable anchors: who the work is for and which situation it addresses.

## 3. Specify buyer and outputs — 02:00–03:35

**Screen action:** Fill the first two fields of the same brief with Stage 1 in the worked example. Point to each concrete artifact and meeting duration. Do not write a second, disconnected example.

**Exact displayed input:**

```text
Business: Fieldwork Studio
Service: Handoff Reset

Buyer:
Independent design studios with 3–12 people that need to organize
handoffs from approved design to implementation.

Deliverables:
- One 60-minute discovery session.
- A map of the current handoff.
- A reusable checklist and a decision log.
- One 45-minute team walkthrough.
```

**Say:**

> The buyer is an independent design studio with three to twelve people. The situation is a handoff from approved design to implementation. That is more useful than “creative teams,” because a reader can recognize whether the service fits their situation. Next, replace the general promise of smoother work with things the buyer will actually receive. Handoff Reset includes a sixty-minute discovery session, a map of the current handoff, a reusable checklist and decision log, and a forty-five-minute team walkthrough. Notice that the meetings and documents are named separately. “Handoff support” would hide those details. A useful check is: could someone point to each promised item when the project is finished? Here, they could. We have described the work without adding a guarantee that it will eliminate delays. But even concrete deliverables can be misunderstood if we leave the scope open.

**Expected visible result — constructed from supplied fictional facts:** Buyer and deliverables are populated. “One workflow,” revisions and exclusions are still to be added from the same source.

**Transition:** The buyer can picture the outputs. Next we show how much work those outputs cover.

## 4. Bound the work and state terms — 03:35–05:05

**Screen action:** Append Stage 2 to the existing brief. Highlight “one workflow,” “two revisions … during the project,” and the difference between duration and start availability.

**Exact displayed input:**

```text
Boundaries and client inputs:
- Covers one workflow.
- Includes two revisions of the documents during the project.
- Client supplies an anonymized recent project and one decision-maker.
- Excludes running client projects, software implementation,
  HR process changes and ongoing support.

Price and timing:
- Fixed price: USD 1,150.
- Project duration: two weeks.
- [TO CONFIRM: current availability / project start date.]
```

**Say:**

> This project covers one workflow. It includes two revisions of the documents during the project. Those words matter: they do not promise unlimited changes, and they do not promise support after the project ends. The client also has a part to play. They supply an anonymized recent project and one decision-maker. Now state the exclusions: running client projects, implementing software, changing HR processes, and ongoing support. These are supplied limits, not limits we guessed. The fixed price is one thousand, one hundred and fifty US dollars, and the project lasts two weeks. That does not tell us the next available start date. We leave availability visibly unconfirmed. If your own source does not state an exclusion, ask about it; do not quietly invent one. A useful brief makes both the promises and the unanswered questions easy to see.

**Expected visible result — constructed from supplied fictional facts:** The same brief now has bounded deliverables, known price and duration, and a clearly marked availability gap. No payment schedule or additional promise has been invented.

**Transition:** Scope and terms are now clear. We still need to check what supports trust and where an interested reader can actually go.

## 5. Use honest proof and contact details — 05:05–06:35

**Screen action:** Append Stage 3. Show the mail link as text only. Do not click it or open an email application. Point to the fictional-address warning and the separate real-world publication check.

**Exact displayed input:**

```text
Permitted proof:
Mira Rowan previously coordinated delivery in a six-person studio
for four years.
No customer testimonials, logos or quantified results are available.

Contact destination:
studio@fieldwork.example
Illustrative mail-link target: mailto:studio@fieldwork.example
Reserved fictional address; not a real inquiry destination.

Before a real page is published:
Replace the example with a real destination the business controls.
A mailto link opens the visitor's mail application; it sends nothing.
A form needs a real submission destination.
An absent destination remains an unresolved publication gap.
```

**Say:**

> For proof, we can say that Mira Rowan coordinated delivery in a six-person studio for four years. We cannot turn that experience into customer testimonials, client logos or measured results. None are available. Next, name the contact destination. Here it is the supplied fictional email address. For your own page, use a real address your business controls and verify the route before publishing. A mail link opens the visitor's mail application. It does not send a message. A form also needs a real submission destination; a box and a button alone do not establish an inquiry system. If that destination is absent, record a publication gap. Our example address is reserved for fiction, so this brief demonstrates the structure. It is not ready to receive real inquiries. We now have enough information to guide the page draft without disguising what is missing.

**Expected visible result — constructed from supplied fictional facts and supplied source notes:** Accurate permitted proof and an explicitly fictional contact are present. The mail-link behavior is source-supplied, not tested during this demonstration. No email or form submission has been sent.

**Transition:** All six fields are present. The final step is to check them together before asking AI to draft anything.

## 6. Check the brief before handing it to AI — 06:35–07:25

**Screen action:** Show the complete final brief in the worked example or the copy-ready text file. Scroll once through the six fields. Show the following instruction beneath it; do not claim to have run it in an assistant.

**Exact drafting instruction, ready to copy:**

```text
Using only the service brief below, draft clear service-page copy.
Preserve the stated scope, price, timing and proof limits. Do not invent
buyers, testimonials, logos, results, guarantees, availability, payment
terms or contact destinations. Keep unresolved items in a separate
“Before publication” list. Do not describe a contact route as working
without verification. This is a fictional teaching case; retain that label
and do not publish it.

[Paste the complete brief here.]
```

**Say:**

> Read the brief once as a buyer. Can you identify the fit, the outputs, the limits, the terms, the evidence and the next contact step? Then read it as an editor. Can every factual claim be traced to the source? Tell the assistant to use only this brief and keep unresolved items in a separate before-publication list. The instruction is ready to copy; we are not running it here. Your finished work today is the brief. Now try the same process with a different service.

**Expected visible result — proposed, not software-validated:** A complete source-bounded brief and a copy-ready instruction. No resulting page copy or AI behavior has been observed.

**Transition:** Quiet Lane has different deliverables and misleading claims. The same six fields will help separate them.

## 7. Transfer to Quiet Lane — 07:25–09:45

**Screen action:** Show the learner worksheet only. Keep the instructor key closed. At 07:45, display a two-minute countdown or hold the worksheet on screen for two minutes.

**Exact exercise input:** The full Quiet Lane source card and bad draft in `03_learner-worksheet.md`. The handout contains everything the learner needs and no repaired brief.

**Say — allow 20 seconds for this cue:**

> Use the Quiet Lane source card to repair the brief. Fill all six fields. Replace unsupported promises with supplied facts, and mark gaps you cannot resolve. Spend two minutes on a first pass. You can use short phrases. Keep the answer key closed until the comparison.

**Practice:** Allow a full two minutes of independent editing, included in the 12-minute estimate. For a recording, retain this two-minute practice window. Learners who need longer may pause; their total elapsed time will then exceed 12 minutes.

**Expected visible result — learner-created, not yet observed:** A six-field Quiet Lane brief with factual claims grounded in the source and explicit gaps where needed.

**Transition:** The comparison will focus on decisions and factual limits, not matching a model paragraph word for word.

## 8. Compare decisions and repair errors — 09:45–11:20

**Screen action:** Reveal only the Quiet Lane model brief from the separate instructor key. Keep the learner worksheet editable beside it. Point to buyer, scope, timing, proof and contact in that order.

**Exact comparison input:** The “Model repaired brief” block in `04_instructor-key.md`.

**Say:**

> Your wording can differ from mine. Check the decisions. The source does not name a specific buyer segment, so mark the intended buyer to confirm. Do not quietly choose a niche. The work is one podcast episode using up to forty-five minutes of supplied audio, with speech cleanup and agreed false-start edits. The outputs are a stereo MP3 and a WAV master. One revision replaces unlimited changes. Other exclusions have not been supplied; mark them to confirm rather than making them up. The price is one hundred and eighty US dollars. Timing is agreed after file review, so “delivery tomorrow” must go. Twelve episodes of the owner's own show are permitted experience; five hundred brands are not. Use the exact supplied email and retain its fictional status. Finally, remove “world-class audio solutions.” Concrete work is more useful here than an unsupported quality claim. Correct any invented or missing fact before polishing the prose.

**Feedback guidance:** Use the rubric in the instructor key. If a learner supplies a plausible buyer, ask which source states it, then help relabel it as an unresolved hypothesis. If they turn missing exclusions into a list of definite exclusions, replace those assertions with confirmation questions. If they retain tomorrow's delivery, return to “timing agreed after file review.” Accept equivalent plain wording that preserves all limits.

**Expected visible result — learner revision, not yet observed:** The learner removes unsupported claims and visibly labels unresolved buyer, exclusions and timing details. The full rubric is for later assessment if needed; do not attempt a lengthy individual scoring session inside this segment.

**Transition:** Once the source facts and gaps are correct, the brief is ready to guide a draft, with publication checks still visible.

## 9. State the next action and collect feedback — 11:20–12:00

**Screen action:** Show the two-item exit response from the worksheet. Allow the final 20 seconds for the learner to respond.

**Say — allow 20 seconds:**

> For your own service, collect these six fields before asking for page copy. Keep gaps visible and verify the contact route before publishing. Mark your brief complete, partly complete or not yet complete, and name the hardest field to finish.

**Learner response — 20 seconds:** Completion status plus one field or “none.” This is feedback, not evidence of sales or course effectiveness.

**Expected visible result — learner-reported, not yet observed:** A completion self-report and a specific clarity signal. An instructor must inspect the actual brief to assess whether the outcome was achieved.
