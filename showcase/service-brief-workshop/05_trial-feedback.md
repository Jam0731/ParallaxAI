# Short trial-feedback plan

## What this pilot can test

Can freelancers or consultants create a six-field brief from a small source card, preserve factual limits and visibly mark missing facts? Which part of the explanation or worksheet makes them hesitate? Is the resulting brief useful for their next drafting task?

This trial does not establish increased sales, market demand, customer outcomes or willingness to pay. No trial has been run and no participant feedback is available.

## Small practical trial

Invite 3–5 representative freelancers or consultants who may soon need a service page and do not identify as prompt engineers. This is a proposed trial size, not a statistically representative sample. No invitation has been sent.

1. **Before the lesson, outside the 12 minutes:** Allow about two minutes for opening or printing the worksheet. Ask the prior-experience question below. Keep the answer key hidden. Use only the supplied fictional cases; participants need not disclose client information.
2. **During the lesson:** Deliver the full sequence and time it with a clock. Preserve the two-minute independent practice window. Note where learners hesitate, request clarification or replace missing facts with guesses. Do not coach them into the answer during the first attempt.
3. **At 09:45:** Capture or inspect the first attempt before revealing the comparison. Score that attempt with the separate rubric. Record corrections after the comparison as assisted revision, not independent success.
4. **At the close:** Collect the two-item, 20-second exit response already in the worksheet.
5. **After the lesson, outside the 12 minutes:** Allow an optional 2–3 minutes for the short form below. If useful, invite learners to try the template on their own service later; that transfer task has no advertised time limit and is not part of the 12-minute lesson.

Do not add a required AI account, tool installation or paid purchase. A text editor or printed worksheet is sufficient.

## Compact feedback form

**Before the lesson**

1. Have you previously written a service brief or used AI to draft a service page? **Neither / Written a brief / Used AI for a page / Both**

**After the lesson**

2. What did you finish during the independent practice? **All six fields / Some fields / Did not start**. Which field, if any, remains unfinished? __________
3. Which specific instruction or field was unclear? **None / Describe it:** __________
4. How useful would this brief be for your next service-page task? **Not useful / Slightly useful / Moderately useful / Very useful / I do not have that task**. What makes you choose that answer? __________
5. What would you need to do next before asking for or publishing a service page? __________

These questions request observations, not compliments. A learner's self-report does not replace inspection of the brief.

## Practical revision rules

- If two or more learners independently invent a buyer or an exclusion, strengthen the missing-fact demonstration and the visible question label in the relevant field. Preserve the rest of the lesson, then retry with new learners.
- If any learner retains fake customer proof or an unsupported deadline, inspect their reasoning and the relevant teaching segment before reuse. One case is a reason to investigate, not proof that every learner will fail.
- If several learners cannot complete a first pass in two minutes, simplify the worksheet's writing burden or increase the advertised practice time. Rehearse again; do not call an overrun a verified 12-minute lesson.
- If learners accurately complete the brief but find it unhelpful, ask what their actual next task requires. Treat usefulness separately from task accuracy.
- Keep the current package as version 1 before making revisions so that results can be compared. These are small-trial operational rules, not statistical validation.
