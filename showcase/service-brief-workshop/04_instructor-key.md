# Quiet Lane — instructor answer key and scoring guide

Keep this file separate from the learner worksheet until the comparison at 09:45. The answer below is one valid wording, not the only acceptable wording.

## Model repaired brief

```text
FICTIONAL TEACHING CASE — NOT FOR PUBLICATION

Business: Quiet Lane
Service description: Editing one podcast episode.

1. Buyer
[TO CONFIRM: intended buyer or buyer segment. The source does not
identify a specific audience.]

2. Deliverables
- Edit one podcast episode using up to 45 minutes of supplied audio.
- Clean speech audio and agreed false starts.
- Return a stereo MP3 and a WAV master.

3. Boundaries and client inputs
- The client supplies the audio; the input is limited to 45 minutes.
- One revision is included.
- [TO CONFIRM: any other important exclusions or scope limits.]

4. Price and timing
- Price: USD 180 for the stated episode-editing scope.
- Timing is agreed after file review.
- [TO CONFIRM after file review: the actual delivery date or turnaround.]

5. Permitted proof
The owner has edited 12 episodes of their own show.
No paying-client testimonials are available.
Do not claim 500 brand clients, client endorsements or measured results.

6. Contact destination
edits@quietlane.example
Reserved fictional address; not a real inquiry destination.
No live contact route has been verified in this exercise.

Before publication of an equivalent real-business page
- Confirm the intended buyer and any additional important exclusions.
- Agree timing after reviewing the files; do not promise tomorrow.
- Replace the fictional address with a real destination controlled by
  the business and verify the intended contact route.
- If a form is chosen, it needs a real submission destination.
  Missing destination information remains a publication gap.
```

## Why these decisions matter

| Decision | Reasoning | Misunderstanding to watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Keep the buyer visibly unconfirmed | A podcast service suggests a task, but the source does not establish a chosen buyer segment. | “For B2B founders,” “for independent coaches,” or another specific niche presented as confirmed. |
| Specify one episode and up to 45 minutes of supplied audio | The limit applies to the input, not a promise that the final episode will be exactly 45 minutes. | Turning the limit into unlimited source audio or a guaranteed final duration. |
| Keep speech cleanup and agreed false starts | “Agreed” matters: the scope does not authorize every editorial change or every possible false-start cut. | Adding extensive content rewriting, music production, recording, publishing or other work as included facts. |
| Name both outputs | A stereo MP3 and WAV master are concrete deliverables. | Dropping one format or inventing technical specifications such as bitrate or sample rate. |
| Preserve one revision; flag other exclusions | One revision is stated. Other exclusions are not supplied. | Keeping unlimited changes, importing Fieldwork's two revisions, or asserting a list of unconfirmed exclusions. |
| Preserve USD 180 and conditional timing | The source fixes the price and says timing is agreed after file review. | Inventing same-day delivery, a two-week duration, or a confirmed date. |
| Distinguish own-show experience from customer proof | Twelve own-show episodes are permitted experience. They do not establish 12 clients or 500 brands. | Recasting the experience as paid-client work, inventing testimonials or calling it quantified customer success. |
| Keep the supplied address and its fictional status | The exact contact is a source fact, but it is reserved for the fictional case. | Treating the example as a live destination or claiming inquiries were sent or received. |
| Remove “world-class audio solutions” | This is vague promotional language with no supplied support. Concrete work communicates the service more usefully. | Believing that deleting only the number 500 makes the rest of the bad draft reliable. |

## Acceptable alternatives

- Different plain wording is fine if it preserves the same input limit, deliverables, revision allowance, price, conditional timing, proof limits and contact status.
- “People who need one podcast episode edited” may be used as a broad task description, but a learner must still show that the intended buyer segment is not supplied. Do not treat that phrase as evidence of a chosen market.
- A learner may list possible exclusions as **questions to confirm**, such as whether additional production work is offered. They must not assert them as established business policy.
- Leaving proof unused is safer than inventing it, although a complete brief should capture the available own-show experience.
- Payment terms, guarantees and technical output specifications need not be invented or exhaustively listed. The central task is an accurate brief, not a full contract.
- The before-publication list may be folded into the relevant fields if the gaps remain conspicuous.

## Short rubric — 12 points

Score the learner's pre-comparison attempt when testing independent transfer. Record later corrections separately; do not count a copied model answer as independent completion.

| Criterion | 2 points: correct and usable | 1 point: incomplete, without invention | 0 points: serious error or absent field |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buyer | Explicitly marks intended buyer unconfirmed; does not invent a niche. | Uses a broad task description but leaves the audience question implicit. | Asserts an unsupported segment, or omits the field. |
| Deliverables | One episode, up to 45 minutes of supplied audio, speech and agreed false-start cleanup, stereo MP3 and WAV master. | Omits one detail while preserving the basic scope and not adding work. | Changes the input limit, adds unsupported deliverables as facts, or omits the field. |
| Boundaries and inputs | Keeps supplied audio and one revision; explicitly marks other important exclusions unconfirmed. | Keeps one revision but leaves another boundary or missing-exclusion note implicit. | Claims unlimited changes, invents definitive exclusions, or omits the field. |
| Price and timing | USD 180; timing agreed after file review; no invented deadline. | Correctly includes only price or timing, without making up the other. | Wrong price, promised tomorrow or another invented deadline, or absent field. |
| Permitted proof | Twelve episodes of the owner's own show; no paying-client testimonials; no fabricated proof. | Omits some usable proof but invents nothing. | Claims brands, paying clients, testimonials or results not in the source, or omits the field. |
| Contact and status | Exact supplied address, visibly fictional, with real destination/route verification still needed before real publication. | Exact address and fictional status retained, but publication check omitted. | Invented address, fictional contact treated as live, unsupported working-form claim, or absent field. |

**Suggested pilot completion rule:** At least 10/12, all six fields addressed, and no invented buyer segment, deliverable commitment, exclusion, price, deadline, customer proof or live-contact claim. This is an instructional rule for this trial, not a validated proficiency threshold. A visible “to confirm” is a successful treatment of a missing fact.

Harmless differences include punctuation, bullet order, “US dollars” instead of “USD,” and “one included revision” instead of “includes one revision.” A serious misunderstanding changes what a buyer could reasonably expect to receive or believe.

## Quick feedback responses

- **“I chose a likely buyer.”** “That could become a hypothesis. Which source confirms it? Until then, label it to confirm.”
- **“I added exclusions to make the brief complete.”** “Completeness includes visible gaps. Turn those new exclusions into questions until the owner confirms them.”
- **“Tomorrow sounds more persuasive.”** “The source says timing is agreed after file review. Persuasive wording cannot create capacity or a delivery commitment.”
- **“The owner has experience, so ‘trusted by brands’ is reasonable.”** “Own-show editing is the evidence we have. Brand-client trust is a different claim.”
- **“The email is in the brief, so inquiries work.”** “It is a reserved fictional address. In a real case, the destination and route must be verified. A mail link opens an application; it does not send anything.”

## Arithmetic and verification boundary

The exercise requires no price arithmetic: the price and counts are supplied facts. The rubric maximum is 6 × 2 = 12 points. The lesson timeline totals 720 seconds. File checks can verify those sums and the presence of the materials; they cannot establish learner completion, teaching effectiveness, contact delivery or a rehearsed runtime.
